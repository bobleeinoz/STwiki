# Construction requirements for reinforcement and tendons

> Scope: AS 3600 Cl 17.2 (reinforcing steel — materials, fabrication,
> bending, surface condition, fixing, lightning protection) and Cl 17.3
> (prestressing ducts, anchorages and tendons — materials, construction,
> tensioning, grouting, unbonded tendons).

## Summary

`[code]` All reinforcement conforms with AS/NZS 4671 (Cl 17.2.1.1). Main
reinforcement is deformed Ductility Class N bar, or Ductility Class L or N
welded wire mesh; fitments are deformed Class N bar, Class L wire/bar
(plain or deformed), or plain Class N bar. Ductility Class L cannot
substitute for Class N without redesign. A protective coating is permitted
only if it doesn't reduce assumed design properties.

## Detail

### Fabrication and bending (Cl 17.2.2–17.2.3)

`[code]` Fabrication tolerances on overall dimension: bars/mesh (not
fitments) −25/+0 mm (≤600 mm length) or −40/+0 mm (>600 mm); fitments −15/+0
mm (deformed) or −10/+0 mm (plain round); cranked column-bar offset −0/+10
mm; sawn/machined end-bearing splice squareness ≤2° over 300 mm. Welding
(including tack welds) conforms with AS/NZS 1554.3.

`[code]` Bending (Cl 17.2.3.1) is cold (around a Cl 17.2.3.3 pin, avoiding
impact/surface damage) or hot (heated uniformly through and beyond the bend,
≤600°C, no quench-cooling; if temperature exceeds 450°C the design yield
strength after bending is taken as 250 MPa). A bar bent-and-straightened or
reverse-bent cannot be rebent within 20 diameters of the prior bend; no
impact bending (hammers). Partially embedded bars may be field-bent if bond
in the embedded portion isn't impaired.

`[code]` Site rebending (Cl 17.2.3.2): using a rebending tool against a flat
surface or a pin ≥ the Cl 17.2.3.3 minimum diameter; no over-bending beyond
~90° from original; scabbling near bars requires extreme care; the bar's
initial bend must be clear of concrete; a bar may be rebent only once at the
same location.

`[code]` Minimum internal bend/hook pin diameter (Cl 17.2.3.3): fitments —
`3db` (500L, R250N), `4db` (D500N); other reinforcement generally `5db`;
reinforcement designed for subsequent straightening/rebending — `4db`
(≤16 mm), `5db` (20–24 mm), `6db` (≥28 mm); epoxy-coated/galvanized
reinforcement — `5db` (≤16 mm), `8db` (≥20 mm).

### Surface condition, fixing, lightning protection (Cl 17.2.4–17.2.6)

`[code]` Surface condition at placement must not impair bond or member
performance; millscale/surface rust alone is not cause for rejection.
Reinforcement (including secondary/positioning steel) is supported within
the Cl 17.5.3 tolerances until the concrete hardens, using bar chairs/
spacers conforming with AS/NZS 2425, specified by strength grade and spacing
for the reinforcement mass and construction loads; tie wire is used unless
the chair has a built-in clip, and must not be exposed within the concrete
cover. Reinforcement used for lightning protection conforms with AS/NZS
1768.

### Materials for ducts, anchorages, tendons (Cl 17.3.1)

`[code]` Duct sheaths/formers must hold their cross-section/profile during
construction. Anchorage quality/properties are established by testing.
Tendons conform with AS/NZS 4672.1 and are never galvanized; non-stress-
relieved hard-drawn high-tensile wire needs ≥3.5% elongation (AS/NZS 4672.1)
for wire winding; plain wire is barred for pretensioning.

### Construction requirements for ducts and anchorages (Cl 17.3.2–17.3.3)

`[code]` Duct surfaces must not impair bond to concrete (outside) or grout
(inside, immediately before grouting); extractable-core withdrawal must not
damage the duct. Ducts are sealed at ends/joints before concrete placement,
and supported/fixed to maintain the profile within Cl 17.5.3 tolerances.
Anchorages are fixed square to the tendon line, per supplier
recommendations, with a grout-tight joint to the duct (and to formwork,
where applicable); their surface condition must not impair concrete bond.

### Tendon fabrication, protection, tensioning, grouting (Cl 17.3.4)

`[code]` Tendons are cut without damaging tendons/ducts/anchorages, never
welded; prestressing bars stay within manufacturing tolerance, not bent in
the threaded portion, and site adjustments are cold (bar raised above 10°C
first if colder). Tendons are protected from stray current/arc/torch
splashes before stressing, and from physical damage/corrosion after; any
coating/wrapping must be inert to both steel and concrete. Surface condition
must not impair bond; surface rust alone isn't cause for rejection unless
visibly pitted. Fixing tolerances follow Cl 17.5.3.

`[code]` Tensioning (Cl 17.3.4.5): force increases at a uniform rate,
transferred gradually; jack force measured to ±3% accuracy; tendon extension
measured and cross-checked against the calculated extension (load-
elongation curve + assumed friction) — disparity >10% is investigated; no
stressing below 0°C ambient. Maximum jacking force: `0.80 fpb Ap`
(pretensioned); `0.85 fpb Ap` (stress-relieved post-tensioned); `0.75 fpb Ap`
(post-tensioned bars/non-stress-relieved). Grouting (Cl 17.3.4.7): ducts
fully filled as soon as practicable after stressing, not below 5°C ambient;
corrosion precautions if grouting is delayed >4 weeks.

`[code]` Unbonded tendons (Cl 17.3.5) are barred except in slabs on ground;
where used, Cl 17.3.4.1–17.3.4.6 apply and corrosion protection is required.

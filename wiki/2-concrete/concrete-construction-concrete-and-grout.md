# Construction requirements for concrete and grout

> Scope: AS 3600 Cl 17.1 — materials, specification/manufacture, handling/
> placing/compacting, finishing, curing/protection, sampling/testing for
> conformance, rejection, and grout requirements.

## Summary

`[code]` Materials and chemical-content limits for concrete and grout follow
AS 1379 (Cl 17.1.1). Concrete is specified as normal-class or special-class
and manufactured/supplied to AS 1379 (Cl 17.1.2); handling, placing,
compaction, finishing and curing must satisfy the Standard's strength,
serviceability, durability and other limit-state requirements. Special-class
concrete specified by strength grade requires project assessment (normal-
class and other special-class concrete may also specify it).

## Detail

### Handling, placing, finishing (Cl 17.1.3–17.1.4)

`[code]` Concrete must be handled/placed/compacted to: limit segregation or
material loss; limit premature stiffening; produce a monolithic mass between
planned joints or member extremities; completely fill formwork, expel
entrapped air, and closely surround reinforcement/tendons/ducts/anchorages/
embedments/fixings; and achieve the specified formed-surface finish.
Unformed surfaces are finished to achieve specified dimensions/falls/
tolerances, cover, and texture.

### Curing and protection (Cl 17.1.5)

`[code]` Curing continues long enough to satisfy strength, serviceability
and stripping requirements, and not less than the Cl 4.4/4.5 durability
minima. Curing is by water application, accelerated curing, or water
retention (sprayed membrane compounds must conform with AS 3799), starting
as soon as practicable after unformed-surface finishing; project
specifications detail member-specific curing. Freshly cast concrete is
protected from rain, running water, freezing and drying before hardening,
and from freezing/drying through the initial curing period.

### Sampling, testing and rejection (Cl 17.1.6–17.1.7)

`[code]` Concrete is assessed for conformance to AS 1379 (Cl 17.1.6.1). By
strength grade (Cl 17.1.6.2): mean cylinder strength `fcm` stays within
AS 1379 limits per grade supplied; under project assessment, slump stays
within tolerance and representative-sample mean strength stays within
AS 1379 limits; cube-to-cylinder conversion is by testing, or ×0.8 absent
data. By other parameters (Cl 17.1.6.3): production/project control methods
must give an operating characteristic curve where a 0.05-proportion-
defective population has ≥50% acceptance probability and a 0.30-proportion-
defective population has ≥98% rejection probability.

`[code]` Plastic concrete (Cl 17.1.7.1) is liable to rejection if slump
deviates beyond AS 1379 tolerance, elapsed mixing-water time exceeds the
AS 1379 interval, or appearance/cohesiveness differs from prior supply.
Hardened concrete (Cl 17.1.7.2) is liable to rejection if it fails Cl 17.1.6,
is porous/segregated/honeycombed or has out-of-limit surface defects, or
fails other Standard requirements. Liable-to-rejection hardened concrete
(Cl 17.1.7.3) may be accepted if calculation or Appendix B testing
demonstrates structural adequacy/intended use is unimpaired; otherwise it is
rejected.

### Grout (Cl 17.1.8)

`[code]` Grout is proportioned for its intended use; grout for prestressing
ducts must be pumpable, low-sedimentation, low-shrinkage, and contain
≤750 mg chloride ion per litre. It is mixed to uniform, lump-free fluidity
and held in motion in an agitation tank before final placement, to prevent
settlement/segregation.
